A market responds to demand backed by purchasing power. Wanting something urgently is different from being able to offer enough to obtain it.

Imagine two people competing to rent a house. One needs a home near their job; the other wants somewhere to spend a few weekends each year. If the second person can pay much more, the market may allocate the house to them. The transaction reveals who can and will pay the most. It does not establish whose need is greater.

This makes the distribution of money part of the mechanism. People with more purchasing power can exert more influence over what gets produced and who receives it. A market can coordinate responses to their preferences effectively while leaving other people’s pressing needs unmet. Distribution and market performance cannot simply be treated as unrelated questions.
