There are reasons to invest this much in another person. You may want more people doing work you care about, someone you can eventually collaborate with, or a successor who can carry a project forward.

A community organizer who helps a newcomer grow may gain a partner capable of leading the next campaign. At an institutional scale, these relationships can help knowledge and responsibility survive the departure of the people who currently hold them.

This helps explain why shared goals matter. A brief conversation can be worthwhile even when two people have little in common. Sustained mentorship asks for more attention, and that investment can make sense when the mentee’s development also advances something the mentor values.

Helping others can also build your standing. People learn that you are someone who makes useful introductions, spots potential, and helps projects happen. They have a reason to bring opportunities and promising people to you, extending what you can accomplish through those relationships.

The benefits can run both ways. Someone with less experience in one area may bring knowledge, relationships, or perspectives the mentor lacks. Experience does not all accumulate in the same person.
