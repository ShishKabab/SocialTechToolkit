The first difficulty appears before anyone takes office: who can recognize the best rulers?

If the existing elite chooses, it may mistake familiarity for excellence. People with the right education, manners, friendships, or family background are easier to recognize as “one of us.” A test can make selection more consistent, but only by choosing which abilities count. An election gives people a voice, but also rewards the ability to win an election. None automatically settles the question of who will govern well.

There is a further problem: people need opportunities to develop the qualities being assessed. If only a narrow group receives education and responsibility, its members may look uniquely qualified because everyone else was denied the preparation.

The classical ideal itself contained exclusions. In the account of aristocracy in Aristotle’s Politics, manual workers are excluded on the assumption that their working lives leave no room for the cultivation of virtue. “The best” was not an invitation for everyone to compete on equal terms.
