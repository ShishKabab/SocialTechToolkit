Direct and representative democracy distinguish who makes decisions. They do not, by themselves, explain who is eligible, who attends, or whether people deliberate together. An assembly and a mass referendum can both make direct decisions while offering different experiences of participation.

AskDistinguish
Who is eligible?All members or a restricted membership
Who participates?Attendees, selected citizens, or elected representatives
How do they interact?A shared assembly, small groups, or a distributed ballot
What authority do they have?Advice, binding decisions, or delegated responsibilities

Athenian democracy is a historical combination of institutions, not a third mutually exclusive type. Its political eligibility was restricted, but its assemblies were not simply gatherings of the wealthy or intimate discussion circles.
