You can also create occasions for these relationships to form. A regular gathering around an interest—a monthly conversation, a reading group, or a small social evening—gives people a reason to meet and return. Each encounter makes it easier to discover shared interests and notice what others can contribute.

Hosting lets people see you doing something: bringing a group together, making newcomers welcome, and following through on an idea. You do not have to be the most experienced person in the room to create something valuable for everyone in it.

It also changes your position in the group. You become someone who knows people and can make useful introductions. Helping two people find each other can be the beginning of a project, a friendship, or a mentoring relationship. Recurring gatherings give these possibilities repeated chances to develop.
