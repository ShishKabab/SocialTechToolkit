On a ship, being good at winning an argument about who should steer is different from knowing how to navigate. The person who understands the winds, currents, and stars might lose the contest for command to someone much better at attracting supporters. This is the problem behind the ancient image of the ship of state: gaining power and knowing how to use it are different abilities.

Aristocracy promises to bring them together. Its appeal is the possibility of entrusting difficult decisions to people who have developed the judgment and character to make them well. In its classical ideal, “best” includes wisdom, integrity, and concern for the community. Intelligence, wealth, or success in a particular occupation would not be enough.

That makes it more demanding than simply putting experts in charge. An expert might know how to achieve a goal while having poor judgment about whether it is worth pursuing—or whose interests it serves.
