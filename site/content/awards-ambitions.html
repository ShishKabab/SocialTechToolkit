The effects reach beyond the winners. An award makes a public statement about what counts as an achievement—and gives everyone watching an example of who they might become.

Think about what your cooking award celebrates. If every winner serves elaborate tasting menus, ambitious cooks may see that as the route to recognition. An award that also honors exceptional, affordable meals could encourage a different ambition: feeding people beautifully on an everyday budget. The choice of winners helps shape what cooks want to become good at.

Distributing recognition is a way of shaping society’s ambitions. What a community celebrates helps determine which contributions people aspire to make. The choice of whom to honor is therefore also a choice about which activities deserve attention.

The same influence can reward the wrong things. An award for impressive announcements may encourage impressive announcements. An award that consistently overlooks collaborators may teach people to seek individual credit. Its stated values matter less than the pattern of achievements it actually celebrates.
