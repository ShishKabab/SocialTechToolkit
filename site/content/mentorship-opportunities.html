Skill alone does not guarantee an opportunity to use it. Someone who has quietly kept a volunteer group running may be ready for a paid leadership role, yet know nobody who would consider them for one. A mentor who has watched their progress can make an introduction and explain why they deserve to be considered.

That introduction carries something a list of contacts does not: another person’s willingness to put their judgment behind you. It can help someone with potential get a chance before they have the track record that would normally open the door.

Sometimes what is missing is confidence. Someone may keep turning down responsibility because they assume they are not qualified. A mentor who knows their work can help them distinguish what they genuinely need to learn from what they are already capable of doing. Encouragement becomes useful when it rests on that understanding.
