If your cooking award makes good choices year after year, its past winners become a reason to trust it. Eventually, it might draw diners to a tiny restaurant they would otherwise have walked past. People try it because they trust the award’s judgment, even though they have never heard of the cook.

An award’s prestige can accumulate over time. Early recipients help establish the award’s reputation; later recipients can benefit from the reputation their predecessors helped build.

But every selection also puts that reputation at stake. If an award starts going to the organizer’s friends, generous sponsors, or people whose achievements do not withstand scrutiny, the audience may stop taking it seriously. The ceremony can continue after the respect has gone.
