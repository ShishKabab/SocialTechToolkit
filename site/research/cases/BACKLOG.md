# Further cases to investigate

These are research leads, **not researched findings or additional completed cases**. Selection should follow the questions, not a desire to collect famous organizations. For each lead, seek operating rules, independent outcomes, unsuccessful participants, and a credible alternative explanation.

## Everyday relationships and small groups

- Benjamin Franklin's Junto: mutual improvement, reciprocity, and selective membership.
- Alcoholics Anonymous alternatives, including SMART Recovery: comparative outcomes and participant fit.
- Men's Sheds: shared activity, belonging, and selection into participation.
- Repair Cafés: skill transmission, recurring gatherings, and volunteer burnout.
- Parkrun: recurring events, distributed hosting, and unequal access.
- Neighborhood block parties: whether recurring invitations produce relationships beyond attendance.
- Death cafés: facilitation, emotional boundaries, and follow-through.
- Study circles in Sweden: learning, civic participation, and public subsidy.
- Balint groups: reflective practice, trust, and evidence of professional benefit.
- Lightning talks at a named technical community: who starts contributing and who stays excluded.
- Mentorship programs with randomized evaluations: matching, intensity, attrition, and dependency.
- Buddy systems in a named workplace: informal sponsorship versus mere onboarding completion.

## Communities and informal institutions

- Burning Man regional groups: principles, formal infrastructure, and founder dependence.
- Transition Town Totnes: local initiatives, sustained participation, and limits of scale.
- Camphill communities: mutual care, work, authority, and safeguarding.
- Israeli kibbutzim before and after privatization: equality, exit, and financial pressure.
- Findhorn's organizational changes: communal identity, financial shocks, and survival.
- A named intentional community that dissolved: admission rules, property, conflict, and succession.
- Time banks with member-level follow-up: exchange activity versus nominal membership.
- Mutual-aid groups after a disaster: rapid mobilization versus durable infrastructure.
- A named rotating savings association: enforcement, exclusion, and member losses.
- Platform cooperatives such as Stocksy and Fairbnb: ownership, actual participation, and market viability.
- A community land trust such as Burlington/Champlain: affordability over time and who gains access.
- A named housing cooperative facing insolvency: governance, capital reserves, and accountability.

## Work and knowledge

- W. L. Gore: lattice organization, sponsorship, and the limits of firm-level performance stories.
- Handelsbanken's decentralized branches: local responsibility, risk, and changes over time.
- Handelsbanken versus a failed decentralized bank: incentives and constraints under stress.
- Spotify's squads: original organizational context versus later imitation.
- Buffer's transparent salaries: recruitment, equity, and changing compensation rules.
- Automattic's distributed work: coordination, hierarchy, and departures.
- Aravind Eye Care: standardization, cross-subsidy, and workforce learning.
- Toyota andon: stopping production, managerial response, and incentives to conceal trouble.
- A failed lean transformation: distinguishing implementation from an unsuitable design.
- Debian maintainer burnout and succession: responsibility without reliable funding.
- Rust governance disputes: foundation, moderation, technical leadership, and resignation.
- Apache project graduation and retirement: community stewardship and institutional life cycles.
- Stack Overflow participation decline: reputation, moderation, platform change, and competing tools.
- Polymath collaboration: open problem solving and credit allocation.
- CERN collaborations: authorship, technical coordination, and decision rights.
- Replication projects and registered reports: prestige, publication incentives, and epistemic improvement.

## Public institutions and movements

- Lobbying campaigns with different outcomes: distinguish access, agenda change, policy adoption, implementation, and pre-existing agreement; compare citizen groups, unions, and industry associations.

- Estonia's digital public infrastructure: institutional prerequisites behind apparent technical simplicity.
- Taiwan's vTaiwan: agenda selection, deliberation, and the gap between proposals and policy.
- Belgium's Ostbelgien citizens' dialogue: permanent sortition and political uptake.
- France's Climate Convention: participant recommendations versus executive implementation.
- British Columbia's citizens' assembly: deliberative design and referendum thresholds.
- Switzerland's referendums: agenda access, turnout, minority rights, and representative institutions.
- Athenian ostracism: elite competition, public protection, and strategic use.
- Venetian selection of the doge: combined lotteries and elections, elite closure, and stability.
- Imperial Chinese examinations: selection, curriculum, social mobility, and narrow incentives.
- Civil-service reform after patronage: capability, accountability, and institutional rigidity.
- US civil-rights movement training networks: churches, disciplined action, and local leadership.
- Otpor and later adaptations: network diffusion, repression, and misleading success narratives.
- Solidarity in Poland: unions, underground coordination, and transition into formal politics.
- Kerala's People's Plan: decentralization, local capability, and participation inequalities.
- Police body-camera trials: compliance, context, and conflicting randomized results.
- Restorative-justice conferencing: victim selection, measured outcomes, and implementation.

## Finance, incentives, and succession

- Advance market commitments for vaccines: demand guarantees, pricing, and additional supply.
- Patent pools such as the Medicines Patent Pool: access, licensing, and product availability.
- The Longitude Board beyond Harrison: multiple awards, verification, and complementary methods.
- Challenge.gov competitions: administrative adoption after winning.
- Social-impact bonds with independent evaluation: outcome measurement and transaction costs.
- GiveDirectly cash-transfer trials: transfers, spillovers, and local market effects.
- Village savings and loan associations: resilience, exclusion, and household bargaining.
- Friendly societies before public insurance: mutual protection and actuarial limits.
- A failed credit union: member control, professional oversight, and concentration of risk.
- Employee ownership trusts: succession, control, debt, and employee voice.
- Foundation-owned firms: long-term stewardship and insulated leadership.
- Berkshire Hathaway succession: delegated capital allocation and institutional continuity.
- Religious orders across multiple successions: training, visitation, discipline, and institutional persistence.
- University endowments and donor restrictions: mission continuity versus adaptation.

## Priority gaps in the completed catalog

1. LiquidO: obtain dated rules, ownership documents, contribution-credit histories, and independent leaver interviews.
2. Microsolidarity: compare participants, nonparticipants, and dropouts over time; include costs and failed crews.
3. Enspiral and Cobudget: distinguish specific ventures and funding rounds; examine distributions, inactive balances, and maintenance labor.
4. Historical cases: compare competing accounts of institutional change with specialist historical scholarship.
5. Everyday scale: add more small, ordinary arrangements with outcome evidence; the catalog currently emphasizes institutions.
6. Geographic breadth: expand beyond European and Anglophone accounts, using locally authored research and untranslated sources where feasible.
